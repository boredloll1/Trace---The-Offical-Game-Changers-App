TRACE — Idea Proposal
GAME CHANGERS · SOCIAL IMPACT INNOVATION

TRACE
Tracking Risks And Combating Exploitation
“It won't happen in one day, but it will happen one day.”
A student-centred initiative to prevent drug-related harm, protect young people from exploitation, and strengthen safety around schools in Delhi.

1. The Problem Statement
Drug trafficking and related exploitation pose concerns for community safety and young people's wellbeing. Students may encounter peer pressure, manipulation, or unsafe situations without knowing how to seek help. Fear of judgement or retaliation can also make it difficult to raise concerns.

Existing law-enforcement, school, and healthcare systems have important roles, but students need clear information about prevention and safe ways to access support.

TRACE aims to address this challenge through a school-centred prevention and referral initiative.

2. The Proposed Solution
TRACE combines a digital information platform with school-based awareness activities and approved support pathways.

Rather than encouraging students to investigate suspected criminal activity, TRACE would help them recognise risks, understand their options, and reach trusted adults or appropriate authorities.

The project would initially be tested in one school before any wider expansion is considered.

3. Key Features
Safe reporting and referral

A school-approved process for raising concerns, with restricted access, clear privacy rules, and review by authorised adults.

Interactive awareness hub

Educational resources, scenario-based activities, and practical guidance on resisting pressure, recognising exploitation, and seeking help.

Trusted support connections

Clear pathways to school safeguarding staff, counsellors, healthcare services, and relevant authorities.

Impact dashboard

Aggregated results showing participation, awareness improvements, knowledge of help channels, and programme effectiveness without exposing students' identities.

4. What Makes TRACE Innovative?
TRACE is designed to connect three elements that are often presented separately: prevention education, safe help-seeking, and responsible referral.

Its proposed innovation is not replacing the police or creating another awareness campaign. It is making the path from recognising a concern to finding appropriate support clearer, more accessible, and measurable.

5. Target Audience
The primary audience is school students, particularly adolescents who need practical prevention education and clear support options.

Secondary stakeholders include school management, parents and guardians, counsellors, healthcare professionals, community organisations, and relevant law-enforcement agencies.

6. Implementation Plan
Research: Consult school staff and relevant professionals to understand existing procedures and local needs.

Prototype: Create sample app screens, educational content, and a demonstration dashboard using fictional data.

Pilot: Test awareness activities and approved support pathways in one school, with appropriate permission and supervision.

Evaluate: Measure awareness, student feedback, accessibility, and safeguarding quality.

Expand: Consider additional schools only if the pilot is safe, useful, and feasible.

7. Expected Social Impact
TRACE aims to improve students' understanding of drug-related risks, increase awareness of trusted support channels, strengthen help-seeking confidence, and improve coordination between schools and relevant services.

Long-term impact would need to be evaluated with suitable partners. A student-led pilot alone cannot establish that drug trafficking across Delhi has decreased.

8. Feasibility and Resources
An initial prototype could be developed using presentation software or no-code tools, with educational content created using credible official resources.

A real reporting system would require school approval, trained adult reviewers, secure data handling, clear referral procedures, and ongoing oversight. These responsibilities must be addressed before collecting actual reports.

9. Sustainability
TRACE could begin with a low-cost digital prototype and school-led awareness activities. Partnerships with schools, public agencies, and community organisations could support future development.

Expansion would depend on evidence of effectiveness, available funding, responsible data management, and the willingness of participating schools to maintain the programme.

10. Risks and Safeguards
The project must account for false accusations, retaliation, privacy breaches, stigma, and the possibility that students may mistake the platform for an emergency service.

Safeguards would include restricted access, minimal data collection, clear explanations of confidentiality limits, review by authorised adults, no public allegations or hotspot maps, and immediate referral to emergency services when necessary.

THE CORE IDEA

TRACE TURNS AWARENESS INTO ACTION.
By combining prevention, safe reporting pathways, and responsible connections to support, TRACE aims to help build safer school communities—one step at a time.
